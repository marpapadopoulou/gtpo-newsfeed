ECB explores link between Tips and Brazil's Pix

Finextra 04 Sep 2026 1 min read
Corporate Banking
Key Insight

Cross-border instant payment interoperability is becoming a geopolitical as well as a technical challenge — European banks should monitor how TIPS linkages evolve, as successful corridors could reshape wholesale and remittance flows, but timelines remain speculative and far from operational impact.

Full Article

The European Central Bank is looking into the possibility of interlinking the Eurosystem's Target Instant Payment Settlement (Tips) with Brazil's Pix system.

In a statement to Reuters, the ECB confirmed that it and Brazil's central bank are carrying out an ongoing preliminary investigation on the possibility of a link. A pilot project could be launched in 2028, says Reuters, citing sources.

The move would reflect a wider effort by the Eurosystem to make it easier for businesses and consumers in Europe to send and receive payments to and from other countries, including remittances.

Last year, the ECB began work to link Tips with India's Unified Payment Interface and other real-time payment rails across Asia.

The euro, Swedish krona and Danish krone are already available for settling payments in Tips, while the Norwegian krone is scheduled to join in 2028 and Iceland's króna will follow.

Launched in late 2020, Pix has proved a runaway success, used by 170 million people, 91% of Brazil's adult population.

In August, the central bank indicated that it was looking to expand the scope of Pix to integrate it with other international systems.

However, the prospect has drawn American scrutiny amid concerns that the state-backed system could impact US firms and even reduce reliance on the dollar. The US government has called the central bank-run service "unfair" and has used it as justification for the imposition of new trade tariffs on Brazil.
